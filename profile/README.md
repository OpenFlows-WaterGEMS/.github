## What Is OpenFlows WaterGEMS

OpenFlows WaterGEMS is Bentley’s water-distribution modeling product. Utilities and consultants open it when the network already exists as GIS and as an operating system: pumps, tanks, valves, customers. OpenFlows WaterGEMS utility model is the hydraulic project. OpenFlows WaterGEMS GIS hydraulics is the link to the map you administer. OpenFlows WaterGEMS water distribution is the study set - fire flow, outage, flushing. OpenFlows WaterGEMS municipal network is the living model of that town or district.

EPANET is the classic engine many people learned on. WaterGEMS is the GIS-centric commercial environment around a calibrated utility model: geodatabase, scenarios, and enterprise reporting. It is not a process-plant Fathom model and not a shop-loop pipe sizer.

Use it on systems the utility owns or a client contracted you to model. Keep personally identifiable service data inside utility policy. Do not sync another city’s geodatabase into a demo if you lack permission.

## Starting with OpenFlows WaterGEMS

Prerequisites: A licensed seat the utility administers, GIS of mains you are allowed to model, and SCADA or demand data for that system.

1. **Prepare:** Extract the district you will study from the geodatabase you keep, with IDs intact.
2. **Configure:** Build OpenFlows WaterGEMS GIS hydraulics so pipes and valves match that extract.
3. **Test:** Run a steady OpenFlows WaterGEMS water distribution solve and compare a few pressures with SCADA you trust.
4. **Refine:** Calibrate, then add fire-flow or flushing on OpenFlows WaterGEMS utility model.
5. **Apply:** Issue the study the operations or planning group asked, on OpenFlows WaterGEMS municipal network.
6. **Maintain:** Sync only after GIS quality control, and archive the model with the extract date.

## Best Uses

- **Utility You Operate:** OpenFlows WaterGEMS municipal network on mains in your GIS.
- **Fire-Flow Letters For That Town:** Hydrants the crew already tests.
- **Isolation And Outage Planning:** Valves that exist in the geodatabase you keep.
- **Consultant On A Contracted City:** Only the GIS the client delivered.
- **Not A Cooling-Water Plant:** Process liquids belong in Fathom.
- **Not A Teaching `.inp` Toy:** Tiny classroom nets can stay in EPANET.
- **Flushing Sequences You Will Actually Run:** OpenFlows WaterGEMS water distribution with field IDs.
- **Demand From Billing You Are Allowed To Use:** Follow utility privacy rules.
- **One Model Per District If That Is How You Operate:** Keep files from becoming a state-sized blob.
- **Calibration Report With Hydrant Dates:** OpenFlows WaterGEMS utility model should cite the tests.
- **Do Not Overlay A Neighbor City:** Permissions first.
- **CMMS IDs Untouched:** OpenFlows WaterGEMS GIS hydraulics should not invent a parallel asset list.
